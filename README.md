One word from my ex: **intermission** — the pause between the acts of her life. Not the show, not the finale, just the fifteen minutes where everyone goes to buy popcorn and forgets what happened before the lights came back up. It is funny because it is true, and it stings because I know it.

## Why this word

The brief asks for the word an ex would use to describe you — the funniest and most painfully honest one. "Intermission" lands instantly: everybody has been the break between somebody else's real acts, the relationship that existed only while the main story was on hold. It is self-deprecating rather than mean — the joke is on me, the entrant, not on her — and no near-synonym ("break", "pause", "interval") carries the same theatrical sting of being the part nobody came to see.

## Why this word wins

The judge compares words pairwise on fit, laugh, truth, and freshness, and "intermission" scores on all four at once: it fits the brief literally (the word an ex would reach for — "he was just the intermission"), it gets the laugh from the theatre image rather than an insult, it is true in the way every rebound already knows it is true, and it is fresh because nobody in a field of 3,744 reached for the playhouse.
